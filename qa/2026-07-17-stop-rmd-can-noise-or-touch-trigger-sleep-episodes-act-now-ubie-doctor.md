Can loud noises or touch during sleep trigger Sleep-Related Rhythmic Movement Disorder (RMD)?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sleep-Related Rhythmic Movement Disorder (RMD) is not typically triggered by loud noises or touch during sleep. Unlike some sleep disturbances that respond to external stimuli, RMD episodes—such as head banging, body rocking, or leg rolling—usually occur spontaneously as a person transitions into sleep, and are not caused by environmental factors like sound or physical contact.

RMD Characteristics: RMD usually happens when falling asleep or during light sleep. The movements are not caused by outside factors like noise or touch.
