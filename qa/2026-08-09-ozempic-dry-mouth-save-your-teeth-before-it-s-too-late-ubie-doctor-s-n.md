If you're taking Ozempic (semaglutide) for type 2 diabetes or weight management, you may have noticed your mouth feels unusually dry. Dry mouth (xerostomia) isn't just uncomfortable—it can raise your risk for tooth decay and gum disease. Here's what you need to know, and how to protect your oral health while continuing your medication.

Why Dry Mouth Leads to Tooth Decay

Saliva plays a critical role in keeping teeth and gums healthy. When saliva production drops:

Acid attacks aren't neutralized, so enamel erodes faster

Bacteria thrive in a drier environment, producing cavity-causing acids

Food debris sticks around longer, fueling plaque formation

Over time, this imbalance can lead to:

White spots or dark pits on tooth surfaces

Sensitivity to hot, cold, or sweet foods

Bleeding or swollen gums

The Protection Plan: Keeping Your Smile Safe

You don't have to choose between blood-sugar control and a healthy mouth. Follow these steps to protect your teeth and gums:

1. Boost Hydration

Sip water throughout the day; carry a refillable bottle.

Swap sugary drinks for plain or flavored water with no added sugar.

Chew sugar-free gum or suck on xylitol mints to stimulate saliva.

Consider over-the-counter saliva substitutes or sprays.

2. Upgrade Your Oral Hygiene

Brush at least twice daily with a fluoride toothpaste.

Use a soft-bristled toothbrush to avoid irritating dry gums.

Floss once daily to remove plaque between teeth.

Rinse with an alcohol-free, fluoride mouthwash to strengthen enamel.

3. Make Smart Diet Choices

Limit sugary, starchy snacks that feed cavity-causing bacteria.

Avoid frequent sips of acidic beverages (citrus juices, sodas).

Incorporate crunchy fruits and vegetables (apple slices, celery) to help clean teeth naturally.

Include calcium-rich foods (yogurt, cheese) to support enamel.

4. Schedule Regular Dental Visits

See your dentist at least every six months for cleanings and exams.

Ask about fluoride varnish or dental sealants to protect vulnerable surfaces.

Let your dentist know you're on Ozempic so they can watch for dry-mouth complications.

5. Monitor Your Mouth Closely

Keep an eye out for early signs of trouble:

Persistent bad breath

White, chalky spots on teeth

Tender or bleeding gums

Additional Tips for Long-Term Oral Health

Sleep with a cool-mist humidifier to keep your mouth from drying overnight.

Quit smoking and limit alcohol, both of which worsen dry mouth and decay risk.

Manage other health conditions (e.g., GERD) that can raise oral acidity.

Consider using a prescription-strength fluoride gel if recommended by your dentist.

Dry mouth and early decay can often be managed at home, but you should speak to a doctor or dentist if you experience:

Balancing Your Health Priorities

Ozempic offers powerful benefits for blood-sugar control and weight management. But if left unaddressed, dry mouth can undermine your oral health, leading to costly and uncomfortable dental problems. By staying hydrated, optimizing your oral care routine, and keeping up with dental visits, you can enjoy the benefits of Ozempic without sacrificing your smile.
