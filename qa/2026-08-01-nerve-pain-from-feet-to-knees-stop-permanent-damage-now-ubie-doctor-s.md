Explanation

Experiencing nerve pain that travels from your feet up into your shins and knees can be unsettling. You might feel tingling, burning, or sharp "electric" sensations that interfere with daily activities—from walking to even standing. This pattern of discomfort often points to a form of peripheral neuropathy, sometimes specifically referred to as neuropathy in shins and knees. Understanding possible causes, evaluation methods, and management strategies can help you take control of your health and find relief.

Common Causes of Radiating Nerve Pain

Peripheral NeuropathyNerve damage in the legs can occur due to:

Diabetes (high blood sugar injures small nerve fibers)

Chemotherapy or certain medications

Vitamin deficiencies (especially B12)

Excessive alcohol use

Nerve Compression or EntrapmentPressure on nerves—often from herniated discs in the lower spine—can lead to:

Sciatica (compression of the sciatic nerve)

Tarsal tunnel syndrome (ankle-level entrapment)

Fibular nerve compression at the knee

Inflammatory ConditionsAutoimmune or inflammatory diseases can inflame nerves and surrounding tissues:

Guillain-Barré syndrome

Chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (CIDP)

Trauma or InjuryDirect injury to the legs, ankles, or knees—such as fractures or surgical complications—may damage nerves.

People describe neuropathic pain in different ways. Common descriptions include:

Tingling or "pins and needles"

Burning or scalding sensations

Stabbing or electric shock–like pains

Numbness or reduced ability to feel temperature changes

Muscle weakness or unsteady gait

Symptoms often worsen at night, making sleep difficult. You may notice decreased sensitivity to touch or an increased sensitivity to things that usually aren't painful (allodynia).

When to Seek Medical Advice

While mild nerve tingling may not be an emergency, you should speak to a doctor if you experience:

Sudden, severe pain or weakness in your legs

Loss of bladder or bowel control

High fever along with spreading numbness or weakness

Rapidly worsening symptoms over days

Signs of infection (redness, swelling, heat) around feet or knees

How Neuropathy Is Diagnosed

Medical History & Physical ExamYour doctor will ask about:

Onset and pattern of pain

Medical conditions (diabetes, autoimmune diseases)

Medication use

Alcohol intake

Neurological Testing

Sensation checks (light touch, vibration)

Reflex testing

Muscle strength evaluation

Laboratory Tests

Blood sugar levels (A1C test)

Vitamin B12 and folate levels

Thyroid function tests

Autoantibody panels

Nerve Conduction Studies & EMGThese tests measure how quickly electrical signals move through your nerves and muscles.

ImagingMRI or CT scans of the spine may detect disc herniation or spinal stenosis compressing nerves.

Treatment Options

Treatment focuses on relieving pain, addressing underlying causes, and preventing further nerve damage. A combination of approaches often works best.

1. Address Underlying Causes

Better blood sugar control for diabetic neuropathy

Nutritional supplementation (B12, folate) if deficient

Reducing or discontinuing alcohol use

Adjusting medications known to damage nerves

2. Pain Management

Over-the-counter pain relievers (acetaminophen, ibuprofen)

Antidepressants (amitriptyline, duloxetine)

Anti-seizure drugs (gabapentin, pregabalin)

Topical agents (lidocaine patches, capsaicin cream)

3. Physical and Occupational Therapy

Stretching and strengthening exercises for legs

Balance training to prevent falls

Custom orthotics or bracing to relieve pressure on nerves

4. Interventional Procedures

Nerve blocks injected near the affected nerve

Spinal cord stimulators for severe, refractory pain

5. Complementary Therapies

Self-Care and Lifestyle Strategies

Consistent self-care helps manage symptoms and supports nerve health:

Maintain healthy blood sugar and blood pressure.

Follow a balanced diet rich in B vitamins, magnesium, and antioxidants.

Stay active: low-impact exercises like walking, swimming, or cycling.

Practice good foot care: inspect daily for cuts, blisters, or infections.

Wear well-fitting shoes with adequate support.

Manage stress through yoga, deep breathing, or meditation.

Avoid smoking and limit alcohol intake.

Preventing Neuropathy in Shins and Knees

You can reduce your risk of worsening or new nerve problems by:

Getting regular checkups if you have risk factors (diabetes, autoimmune disease).

Exercising moderately at least 150 minutes per week.

Keeping weight in a healthy range to lessen joint and nerve compression.
