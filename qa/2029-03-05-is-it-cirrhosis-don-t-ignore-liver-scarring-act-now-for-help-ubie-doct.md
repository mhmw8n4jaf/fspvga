Is it Cirrhosis? Why Your Liver is Scarring & Medically Approved Next Steps

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Cirrhosis is advanced liver scarring that develops over years from alcohol use, chronic hepatitis B or C, fatty liver tied to obesity or diabetes, autoimmune disease, genetic conditions, or bile duct problems. Early stages are often silent, but it can progress to jaundice, fluid buildup, easy bleeding, and confusion.

Doctors diagnose cirrhosis using blood tests, imaging or elastography, and sometimes a biopsy. Treatment focuses on urgent evaluation of red-flag symptoms, addressing the underlying cause, strict alcohol avoidance, weight and metabolic control, a low-salt diet, safe medication use, and regular screening for liver cancer and varices. Transplant is reserved for advanced disease.

When the liver is repeatedly injured — from alcohol, viruses, fat buildup, or other causes — it tries to repair itself. Over time, that repair process creates scar tissue. As more scar tissue forms, healthy liver tissue is replaced. This makes it harder for the liver to function normally.

Early liver damage may be reversible. Advanced cirrhosis, however, is generally permanent. That's why recognizing it early is so important.

What Are the Symptoms of Cirrhosis?

Early cirrhosis often causes no noticeable symptoms. This is why many people don't realize they have liver scarring until it's advanced.

As the disease progresses, symptoms may include:

Early Symptoms

Fatigue

Weakness

Loss of appetite

Nausea

Mild abdominal discomfort

Unexplained weight loss

More Advanced Symptoms

Yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice)

Swelling in the abdomen (ascites)

Swelling in the legs

Easy bruising or bleeding

Itchy skin

Confusion or trouble concentrating (hepatic encephalopathy)

Vomiting blood or black stools (a medical emergency)

Can Cirrhosis Be Reversed?

This depends on the stage.

Early-stage cirrhosis: Damage may be slowed or partially improved if the underlying cause is treated.

Advanced cirrhosis: Scarring is typically permanent, but progression can often be slowed.

The most important step is identifying and addressing the root cause.

If you're concerned about cirrhosis, here's what experts typically recommend:

1. Speak to a Doctor Promptly

Cirrhosis can lead to life-threatening complications. If you have symptoms like:

Vomiting blood

Severe abdominal swelling

Confusion

Yellowing of the eyes

Seek urgent medical care.

Even without emergency symptoms, schedule an appointment with your primary care provider or a liver specialist (hepatologist).

2. Treat the Underlying Cause

Treatment depends on what caused the liver damage:

Alcohol-related cirrhosis: Complete alcohol cessation

Hepatitis B or C: Antiviral medications

Fatty liver disease: Weight loss, diabetes control, cholesterol management

Autoimmune disease: Immune-suppressing medications

Stopping the source of injury is the single most important step.

3. Lifestyle Changes That Protect Your Liver

These changes are medically recommended and can significantly improve outcomes:

Avoid alcohol completely

Maintain a healthy weight

Eat a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and lean proteins

Limit salt (especially if you have swelling)

Exercise regularly

Avoid unnecessary supplements or herbal products without doctor approval

Some supplements marketed for "liver detox" can actually worsen liver damage.

4. Regular Monitoring

If you have cirrhosis, routine follow-up is essential. Doctors may monitor for:

Liver cancer (via ultrasound every 6 months)

Esophageal varices (enlarged veins that can bleed)

Worsening liver function

Monitoring allows early treatment of complications.

5. Medications for Complications

Depending on symptoms, your doctor may prescribe:

Diuretics (for fluid buildup)

Beta blockers (to reduce bleeding risk)

Lactulose (for confusion due to toxin buildup)

These treatments don't cure cirrhosis but can manage serious risks.

6. Liver Transplant (For Advanced Cases)

In severe cirrhosis where liver function is failing, transplant may be considered. This is typically reserved for advanced disease after careful evaluation.

While this sounds overwhelming, many people with cirrhosis live for years with proper care and monitoring.

When Should You Be Especially Concerned?

Seek immediate medical attention if you experience:

Black, tarry stools

Severe confusion

High fever with abdominal swelling

Sudden severe abdominal pain

These can signal life-threatening complications.

The Bottom Line on Cirrhosis

Cirrhosis is serious — but it's not hopeless.

It develops slowly, often silently. The earlier it's detected, the more options you have to slow progression and prevent complications.
