Is there any evidence suggesting that ginger could trigger migraines in some individuals?

Ginger is not a known migraine trigger, and there is no strong scientific evidence linking it to migraine attacks. In fact, ginger is often studied for its potential to relieve migraine symptoms, such as nausea and pain. However, individual reactions can vary. Some people may be sensitive to ginger or experience side effects like heartburn or digestive upset, which could indirectly contribute to a headache. If you consistently notice migraines after consuming ginger, it may be worth tracking your intake and speaking with a healthcare provider.

Because migraines can have many overlapping causes—diet, hormones, stress, sleep, or underlying conditions—guessing the trigger on your own can be frustrating and unreliable.
