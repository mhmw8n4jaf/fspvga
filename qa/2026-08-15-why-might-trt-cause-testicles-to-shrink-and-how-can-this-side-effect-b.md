Why might TRT cause testicles to shrink, and how can this side effect be managed?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) can cause testicular shrinkage (testicular atrophy) as a common side effect. Here's why it happens: when a man takes external testosterone, the brain detects high hormone levels and stops sending signals (LH and FSH) to the testicles. Without these signals, the testicles reduce their own testosterone production and shrink over time.

The good news is that this side effect is manageable. Treatment options include:

Adjusting TRT dosage to lower levels

Adding hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) to mimic the brain's natural signals and keep the testicles active

Using selective estrogen receptor modulators (SERMs) like clomiphene to support natural production

TRT gives the body extra testosterone that comes from outside, and the brain notices this and thinks, "We have enough now!" Because of this, the brain stops sending the usual signals (through hormones like LH and FSH) to the testicles to make their own testosterone. When the testicles don't have to work as hard, they can shrink in size.

This shrinkage happens because the body is trying to be efficient by not making more testosterone that it doesn't need. To manage this side effect, doctors often adjust the dosage of testosterone or add human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) to the treatment. hCG acts like a signal to the testicles, telling them to keep working and stay the right size.

Some treatments may also include periodic breaks or combining other medications so that the natural production of testosterone is supported. This way, a balance can be maintained while still getting the benefits of TRT.
