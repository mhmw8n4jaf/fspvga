# 6 Easy Ways to Switch Up Your Workout Right Now

Maybe you’ve been following the same upper body–lower body split since college. Or maybe the thought of starting your old standby routine once more makes you want to claw your eyes out.

Whatever the reason may be, you’ve hit workout fatigue. And once that sets in, it’s hard to motivate yourself to hit the gym and start sweating. As a result, you might see your gains start to dwindle, or even plateau.

“Boring or miserable workouts can produce physical results,” says Men’s Health training advisor David Jack. “Butif they aren't personally satisfying, those gains won't last long, and neither will your consistent fitness lifestyle.”

Switch up your workout: Try new moves

It’s the most obvious example: If your workout is getting stale, simply add in new moves to keep it fresh.

“We are always going to resort to the things we like, the things we know, and the things we’re comfortable with, so you need to make an effort to sprinkle in some things you need to try,” says Jack.

That means give some things you might not do all the time a shot: think a new sport or activity, a new kind of exercise—hip thrusts, anyone?—or even a new workout class, like spin, boxing, or bootcamp.

Switch up your workout: Try the same moves differently

[Ways to Switch Up](https://insulin-pathways.hbsnzj.com/)

You don’t always need to reinvent the wheel—just tweak it a bit. So don’t feel like you need to completely give up an exercise you like.

That means switch an exercise with a new variation instead of scrapping it altogether, says Tony Gentilcore, C.S.C.S., owner of CORE in Boston, Massachusetts. This is an especially great tip if the reason you’re looking for a change is because you’re dealing with an injury caused by repetition.

Say, for instance, the back squats hurts your lower back. Instead, try the front squat or a goblet squat, he says. Changing from dumbbells to barbells or vice versa can help mix things up, too.

Switch up your workout: Manipulate sets and reps

[Ways to](https://pepenails.com/bqcx7p/)

Okay, so you want to keep some old standby exercises in your routine. That’s fine, but you need to continually challenging your body with them if you want to see results.

That’s where changing up your sets and reps comes in. Most people tend to gravitate to three sets of 10. So instead, try to do 10 sets of three, Gentilcore. Just up your weight accordingly.

And don’t forget drop-sets and the last set to failure—both will stimulate strength and growth.

Switch up your workout: Change up your rest

Lots of times, guys aren’t so good with sticking to a rest schedule—they often find themselves spending minutes chatting with a buddy, getting a drink of water, or scrolling through Instagram instead, Gentilcore says.

So if you’ve been taking long rests, shorten it up: “Hold yourself accountable for 50 seconds of rest,” says Gentilcore. This generally equates to a work-rest ratio of 1:1, which help you build muscle endurance, making your muscles more resistant to fatigue.

Switch up your workout: Manipulate the tempo

Gentilcore recommends utilizing the time under tension theory—how long a muscle is under strain during a set—to mix things up. Usually, we tend to try to get the move done as quickly as possible, but that’s not the most effective way to challenge your muscles.

Take five seconds to do the eccentric or lowering part of a squat, and then five seconds to come back up, which is the concentric phase, he says. This is a great way to increase the time under tension in your workouts.

Switch up your workout: Add in pauses

To make an exercise more challenging, Gentilcore suggests adding in a pause before you initiate the concentric part of the movement. For example, if you’re doing a push-up, lower yourself down and pause before you push back up. This takes momentum out of play, which makes your muscles work even harder.
