Explanation

The cheek under the eye is supplied by branches of the trigeminal nerve, which can become irritated or affected by various factors, including:

Nerve irritation or compression: Pressure from muscle tension, injury, or swelling can cause tingling. Sinus infections or allergies: Inflammation in the sinuses or allergic reactions can cause tingling or numbness in nearby facial areas. Shingles (herpes zoster): Early stages may cause tingling or burning before a rash develops. Trigeminal neuralgia: A condition causing sharp or tingling facial pain, sometimes affecting the cheek area. Dry skin or irritation: Skin conditions or exposure to irritants can cause mild tingling. Migraine aura: Some migraines cause tingling sensations in the face before the headache starts. If tingling persists, worsens, or is accompanied by other symptoms like rash, pain, or weakness, consult a healthcare provider to identify the cause and get appropriate treatment.
