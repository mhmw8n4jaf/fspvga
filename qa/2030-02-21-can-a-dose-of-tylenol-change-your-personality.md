# Can a Dose of Tylenol Change Your Personality?

Taking Tylenol can ease your aches, but it also may blunt another kind of feeling: empathy.

According to new research in Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience, acetaminophen can decrease your ability to understand other people’s pain.

The researchers discovered that people who took 1,000 milligrams (mg) of acetaminophen—the pain-relieving ingredient in Tylenol—rated others’ agony after a traumatic event as less severe than those who took a placebo did.

Acetaminophen may suppress activity in two parts of your brain called the anterior insula and the dorsal anterior cingulate cortex, both of which are involved in the sensation of and emotional reaction to physical pain, says lead study author Dominik Mischkowski, Ph.D., of the National Institutes of Health.

And that can blunt your ability to feel empathy, he says, which might make you come off as insensitive or uncaring.

For instance, instead of inquiring how your coworker’s family is handling the loss of a loved one, you might be inclined to simply say, “I’m sorry about your loss” and move on. It’s the difference between a more heartfelt response and a surface-level response.

The empathy effects were clear after taking 1,000 mg of acetaminophen, which is one adult dose.

It’s likely the meds will diminish your empathy for as long as they last in your body, generally four to six hours, Mischkowski says.
