Certain prescription medications commonly taken by seniors can disrupt sleep, sometimes creating a cycle where additional sleep aids are needed. Drugs such as beta-blockers, corticosteroids, diuretics, antidepressants, and decongestants may interfere with falling asleep, staying asleep, or achieving restful sleep quality.

If you or a loved one is experiencing sleep difficulties, it's important to understand whether medications, an underlying condition, or another factor may be the cause. Explanation

Prescription medications can indeed cause sleep problems in seniors. As people age, their bodies process drugs differently, which can affect sleep. One of the references highlights that seniors are at risk of starting sleep medications due to various factors, including health conditions and medication use. Some also discuss how aging and drug use can impact sleep, indicating that certain medications might disrupt sleep patterns. Additionally, they also note that both new-onset and chronic sleep issues in seniors can be linked to medication use.

Key Points:

Age-related Changes: As people get older, their bodies change, affecting how drugs work and potentially leading to sleep issues.

Medication Effects: Some drugs can interfere with sleep, making it hard for seniors to fall or stay asleep.

Increased Sleep Medication Use: Seniors may start using sleep medications due to these issues, as noted in the studies.
