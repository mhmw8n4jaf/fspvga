# How Your Expense Account Clogs Your Arteries

Non-stop schmoozing with clients over dinner can be more than a mental strain—it might take a toll on your heart, too, a new study from the American College of Cardiology suggests.

In the study, researchers analyzed people’s diets and broke them up into three types: a Mediterranean diet, full of fruits and vegetables, lean proteins, and healthy fat; a Western diet, characterized by refined cereals, dairy, and pre-made foods like frozen dinners; and the newly-coined “social-business” diet, which was even heavier on pre-made foods, but also included a lot more sweet drinks, processed snacks, booze, and restaurant meals.

(The researchers named it the social-business diet because the people who ate that way tended to be busy, high-ranking careerists who dined out frequently for work.)

The scientists discovered that the people who ate the social-business diet were 8 to 11 percent more likely to have highly clogged arteries—which can eventually cause a heart attack or stroke—than the other groups.

That’s probably because they tended to eat more, says study author Valentin Fuster, M.D., Ph.D., physician-in-chief at the Mount Sinai Hospital in New York.

On average, the social-business dieters ate nearly 500 more calories per day than people who ate a Mediterranean diet, and 140 more calories per day compared to those who ate a Western diet.

It’s easy to eat too much when you’re often traveling for work or wining and dining clients. On-the-go foods like fried and breaded meats, soda, and starchy sides tend to be high in calories, Dr. Fuster says, and recent research shows that a majority of restaurants pack more than a recommended serving size on your plate.

That overeating can lead to weight gain, which raises your risk for high cholesterol and high blood pressure, in turn leading to plaque buildup that can clog your arteries, according to the American Heart Association.

If you must eat out often for work, follow these steps to avoid overdoing it:

Drink two glasses of water before your meal and sip at least one more glass as you eat, says Men’s Health nutrition advisor Alan Aragon, M.S. This can help you feel full faster and prevent you from craving dessert later, he says.

Avoid the appetizers, which tend to be deep-fried and full of empty calories. (Or get a light salad if you can’t wait for the main course.)
