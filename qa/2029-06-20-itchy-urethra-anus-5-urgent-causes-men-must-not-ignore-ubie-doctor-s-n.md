Internal Medicine

An itchy urethra and anus in males is most often caused by sexually transmitted infections (STIs), fungal skin infections like jock itch or candidiasis, or exposure to irritants such as soaps, laundry detergents, or hygiene products that trigger inflammation. Less commonly, conditions like hemorrhoids, pinworms, or urinary tract infections may be responsible. Because symptoms often overlap between causes, identifying the exact source usually requires a closer look at accompanying signs—such as discharge, rash, burning during urination, or visible irritation.

Since the underlying cause directly determines the right treatment—whether that's antibiotics, antifungal creams, or simply switching products—guessing can delay relief and, in the case of untreated STIs, lead to more serious complications. Explanation

There are several reasons why a man might feel an itch in his urethra (the tube that carries pee) and his anus (the end of the digestive tract). One possibility is an infection. For example, infections that are passed during sex can lead to urethritis, which is an irritation or infection of the urethra. Germs like Chlamydia or other bacteria can be responsible for this and may make you feel itchy. Another possibility is a fungal infection. Fungal infections in the groin area can sometimes cause a rash and may spread or cause discomfort near the urethra, similar to a condition called tinea cruris. Also, irritation from chemicals, soaps, or other substances can cause itching in the anal area, a condition known as pruritus ani. It is important to remember that each cause needs a proper look by a doctor to know exactly why the itching is happening so that the right treatment can be given.
