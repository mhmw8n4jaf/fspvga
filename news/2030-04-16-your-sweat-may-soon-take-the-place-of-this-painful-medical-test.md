# Your Sweat May Soon Take the Place Of This Painful Medical Test

Every day, an estimated 6,800 new peer-reviewed academic articles are published. That’s a whole lot of science to wade through—but don’t fret. We’ll do the legwork for you, each and every morning. Here’s your daily dose of the latest discoveries from journals, research institutions, and news outlets from around the world.

Sweat For Science Your sweat may soon play a key role in monitoring diabetes, a new study in the journal Science Advances discovered. Scientists have developed a sensor that can accurately monitor your blood sugar levels using tiny samples of your sweat. That’s important, since the current way to measure blood sugar involves pricking your fingertip with a lancing device to squeeze out a drop of blood, which is then tested. Not surprising, lots of diabetics find this testing method painful, so the sweat sensor could be a welcome innovation.

Seek Praise Receiving compliments about your running can help you perform better, researchers from the University of Nevada discovered. When the researchers told runners their V02 max—a top measure of cardiovascular fitness and aerobic power—was better than that of the rest of the group, their V02 max increased by 3 percent.

Boost Your Willpower Trying to lose weight? Eating with your buddies might not be doing you any favors, research presented at the American Heart Association Meeting discovered. When those trying to lose weight ate with others, they had about 60 percent chance of a diet lapse. What’s more, they had a 60 percent chance of overeating when they were dining at a restaurant. Bring On the Coffee Here’s one more reason to drink your daily cup of coffee: Caffeine may boost an enzyme in your brain that protects against dementia, researchers from Indiana University at Bloomington found. The enzyme, called NMNAT2, may guard neurons in your brain from stress, and may also fight the development of proteins called tau, which accumulate in the brain as plaque and have been linked to Alzheimer’s disease. Get Good Grades Choose your weekend wind-downs wisely: College students who smoked moderate to high amounts of marijuana and drank the same amounts of alcohol earned significantly lower GPAs than those who didn’t use the substances as frequently, a study in Plos One found. And students who cut down their use over time performed better academically than those whose use remained stable.
