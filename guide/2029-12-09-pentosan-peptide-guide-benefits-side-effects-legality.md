What is Pentosan?

Pentosan is a polysaccharide compound of pentose sugar and is derived from five-carbon sugars such as xylose. It is naturally found in various materials such as barley, sugarcane bagasse, pinewood, and peanut shells.

A semi-synthetic polysulfated form of Pentosan, namely Pentosan polysulfate sodium, is sold and thus used as a drug for pain relief purposes. It is mainly isolated from beech trees through sulfate esterification. The average molecular weight of Pentosan polysulfate is around 5700 Da (1).

How Does Pentosan Peptide Work?

Pentosan functions as a pain-mediating substance and has been proven to affect several other mechanisms of the human body.

While these mechanisms may sound complex initially, I will try to make them simple for you!

Pentosan is also called a weak heparinoid, which means that it acts as an anticoagulant. It does so by inactivating thrombin, primarily responsible for blood clotting in the body. This prevents the formation of thrombus in the blood vessels and thus also acts as a blood thinner.

Chondroitin-4-sulfate is an important component of cartilages and joints which is broken down by the enzyme chondroitin-4-sulfatase. Pentosan can inhibit chondroitin-4-sulfatase, which then allows Chondroitin-4-sulfate to stimulate the production of new cartilage and act as an anti-inflammatory substance (2).

One of the studies done on animals showed that Pentosan not only promotes the formation of cartilage, it also stimulates the synthesis of proteoglycans by chondrocytes and makes them bind to connective tissue matrices. Proteoglycans are an essential component of the synovial fluid of joints, and it helps the joint withstand compressional forces (3).

Pentosan further stimulates the production of hyaluronic acid, the chief component of hyaline cartilage, and is present in the joint as a lubricator. It also improves skin quality by reducing wrinkles and helps wounds to heal faster (4).

B-amyloid plaques are misfolded proteins that are present in the brain of patients suffering from Alzheimer’s disease, and these plaques have toxic effects on the endothelial cells of the brain. In such cases, Pentosan has been reported to modify the size and decrease the number of amyloid aggregates (5).

Pentosan also contributes to the development of molecules that protect the blood-brain barrier. Last but not least, some studies performed on mice show that Pentosan has also been effective in curbing prions disease (6).

Pentosan acts as an anticoagulant and blood thinner, due to which a major side effect of this peptide is linked with hemorrhage. These are most often observed during dental surgery, where gum bleeding is prolonged. Excessive bleeding during other surgeries is also a major risk factor.
