How can auditory hallucinations be distinguished from other types of hallucinations?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Auditory hallucinations are the experience of hearing sounds, voices, or noises that aren't actually present. They differ from other types of hallucinations, such as visual hallucinations, which involve seeing things that aren't there, or tactile hallucinations, which involve feeling sensations without a physical cause. Auditory hallucinations can range from simple sounds like buzzing or ringing to complex voices speaking words or full sentences.

Explanation

Auditory hallucinations are when someone hears sounds or voices that others do not hear. These sounds can be clear or unclear, and they might seem to come from inside or outside the person's head.

Here's how auditory hallucinations differ from other types:

Auditory vs. Visual Hallucinations: Auditory hallucinations involve hearing, while visual hallucinations involve seeing things that aren't there.

Auditory vs. Other Senses: Other hallucinations might involve taste, smell, or touch, but auditory hallucinations are specifically related to hearing.
