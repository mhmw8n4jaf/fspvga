What Is Traditional Primary Care?

Traditional primary medical care is a standard model of healthcare used by most hospitals, clinics, and private practices. Medical services are provided through insurance-based billing systems and includes annual physicals, treatment of common illnesses or injuries, chronic disease management, and vaccinations via in-office visits.

How Most Americans Access Healthcare

Most Americans access healthcare through a primary physician. An in-office visit is where annual physical exams, and treatments for common illnesses and injuries are offered. Because healthcare is often prohibitively expensive, most Americans use medical insurance offered through their employers or through government funded programs.

Traditional primary care is largely based on insurance-billed programs. Patients receive care and the primary physician bills the insurance company; the patient pays for any non-covered services, usually after an annual deductible is exhausted. Traditional primary care doctors accept insurance or only insurances accepted by a network of providers. As such, primary care doctors have a large load of patients and obtaining an appointment can take weeks or months.

Short appointments, longer wait times

Due to a primary care doctor’s patient load, it may take a while before an appointment can be scheduled. It is not uncommon for a patient to wait weeks or months before an available time is available on the doctor’s schedule. Additionally, once a patient arrives for an appointment, the actual time spent with the doctor is very short.

Reactive vs. proactive care

When patient appointments are short due to the doctor’s back-to-back appointment schedule, primary care doctors tend to be reactive: treating illnesses as they arise instead of focusing on preventive and proactive care.
