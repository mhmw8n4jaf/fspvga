How to Get Rid of Hip Dips

Hip dips—technically known as "violin hips" or "trochanteric depressions"—are inward curves along the sides of your hips. They're a normal part of human anatomy, determined largely by your bone structure, muscle mass, and fat distribution. While you can't change your pelvic bone shape, you can soften the appearance of hip dips through targeted exercises, nutrition, posture, wardrobe choices, and, for those seeking quicker or more dramatic results, cosmetic procedures.

What Causes Hip Dips?

Pelvic shape: The width and angle of your hip bones create natural indentations.

Muscle development: Low muscle mass around the glutes, outer thighs, and hips can make dips more noticeable.

Fat distribution: Genetics influence where your body stores fat—some store less fat over the greater trochanter (the bony point on your hip), enhancing the dip.

Hip dips are neither unhealthy nor abnormal. Cosmetic and Minimally Invasive Options

If you're seeking faster or more pronounced results, consider consulting a board-certified plastic surgeon. Discuss benefits, risks, cost, and recovery for procedures such as:

Fat grafting (lipofilling)Using your own fat—harvested via liposuction from another area—to fill hip dips. According to Mofid MM & Mofid VC (2019), gluteal fat grafting is a commonly performed procedure with evolving techniques to enhance safety and outcomes.

Dermal fillersHyaluronic acid or other fillers can be injected to smooth dips. Results last up to 18 months but require periodic maintenance.

ImplantsSolid silicone implants placed near the hips to round out the silhouette. Permanent but involve a longer surgical and recovery process.

Key considerations:

All surgical and minimally invasive procedures carry risks: infection, asymmetry, scarring, or changes in sensation.

Recovery time varies. Fat grafting and implants may require several weeks off strenuous activity.

Costs range widely based on location, surgeon experience, and technique.

Always choose a qualified specialist and verify board certification. Request before-and-after photos, read patient reviews, and ask about revision policies.

Realistic Expectations

Muscle and fat take time to change. Non-surgical approaches often require 8–12 weeks of consistent effort to see noticeable improvements.

Cosmetic procedures can deliver quicker results but come with higher cost, downtime, and medical risk.

Hip dips may never disappear entirely—embracing them as a normal body feature can be just as empowering.
