# Style Editors Say This One Thing Makes or Breaks a Flannel

Our product picks are editor-tested, expert-approved. We may earn a commission through links on our site. Why Trust Us?

FOR A LONG time, I treated flannel like a throwaway shirt. Something you grab because it’s easy, comfortable, and impossible to screw up. Need to throw on a decent office look without putting in too much effort? Flannel. Matcha runs for a mid-day pick-me-up? Flannel. Weekend errands where I might bump into someone I know? Still flannel. It felt like the safest buy in menswear—soft, warm, and timeless.

Over the years as a style editor at Men’s Health, I’ve tested so many of them, and yet, I realized how many flannels quietly suck. Some feel great in the store and then turn stiff after one wash. Others are cozy but so boxy they make you look like you borrowed your dad’s shirt. And a few just never fit quite right, so they sit in your closet until you eventually stop acting like you’ll wear them again. That’s when it dawned on me: shopping for a flannel isn’t as easy as we pretend it is. The good ones earn their spot. The bad ones just take up hanger space.

Start by Defining How You’ll Wear It

Before you think about fabric or pattern, you need to be honest about how this flannel will fit into your life. A shirt you plan to wear casually on weekends should feel soft and broken-in, while one meant for layering needs enough structure to sit cleanly under a jacket. If you’re treating flannel as a lightweight jacket substitute, weight and durability matter far more. When you define the job upfront, you avoid buying a shirt that technically works but never feels right.

Fabric Is the Foundation of a Good Flannel

The biggest difference between a great flannel and a forgettable one is the fabric. Well-made flannel feels soft without being flimsy and substantial without being stiff. Midweight brushed cotton tends to strike the best balance, offering warmth and breathability while holding its shape over time. Thin flannels often feel nice at first but lack longevity, while overly rigid ones rarely soften enough to become comfortable. If it doesn’t feel good the first time you put it on, it won’t magically improve later.

Fit Should Feel Natural, Not Forced

Flannel is supposed to move with you, not fight your body. A good fit sits close without pulling across the chest, doesn’t tug in the arm, and drapes cleanly at the hip. Overly slim flannels may look sharp on the hanger but feel restrictive once you’re reaching for something on a high shelf or carrying a backpack. And then you have boxy cuts, which can make you feel and look sloppy. The goal is to find one that you can wear buttoned up, open over a t-shirt, or layered under a winter coat without feeling stiff, tight, or uncomfortable.

Construction Details Reveal Long-Term Quality

The signs of a well-made flannel are subtle but important. Strong stitching at the shoulders and side seams helps the shirt keep its shape, while solid buttons and reinforced stress points prevent early wear. Even small details, like how a plaid lines up at the chest pocket, signal whether a brand took care during production. These elements don’t change how the flannel looks on day one, but they determine how it holds up after months of wear.

Pattern Choice Affects How Often You’ll Wear It

Plaid is timeless, but not every plaid is versatile. Muted colors and classic checks tend to integrate more easily into an existing wardrobe, while loud or trendy patterns can feel dated quickly. A flannel should work with the jeans, chinos, and jackets you already own, not demand an entirely new outfit to make sense. The more effortless it feels to style, the more value you’ll get from it.

The best flannels improve with age. They soften over time, mold to your body, and become the shirt you reach for without thinking. When you’re shopping, it helps to imagine how the flannel will look after a dozen washes and a full season of wear. If it feels versatile enough to wear casually and polished enough to layer, it’s likely a smart long-term addition rather than a short-lived impulse buy. When you approach flannel with the same intention you’d give any other wardrobe staple, you end up with fewer shirts and far better ones.
