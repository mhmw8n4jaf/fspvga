What is Noopept?

Noopept (N-phenylacetyl-L-prolylglycine ethyl ester) is a synthetic nootropic compound with cognitive enhancing effects. It is often associated with the racetam family of nootropics, though it has a widely distinct mechanism of action.

Noopept is composed of a dipeptide structure that includes proline, glycine and a phenylacetyl group, which is commonly used as a cognitive enhancer, booster and neuroprotective agent. Noopept is also known as Omberacetam, a lesser known alternative name for the compound.

How Does Noopept Work?

Noopept works by regulating glutamate receptors, specifically AMPA and NMDA receptors, which are crucial for synaptic plasticity, learning, and memory formation. By enhancing glutamate signaling, it improves neuronal communication, leading to improved memory retention.

Additionally, it regulates acetylcholine activity, another key neurotransmitter involved in attention and memory retention, which is also known to help with age related cognitive decline. This mechanism is similar to racetams but significantly more powerful, requiring much lower doses for noticeable effects.

It stimulates the production of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) and nerve growth factor (NGF), two essential proteins that promote neuronal cell growth, repair, and survival. By reducing oxidative stress and inflammation in the brain, it helps protect neurons from damage, delaying neurodegenerative conditions such as Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s disease and mild cognitive impairments of vascular and traumatic origin.

Side Effects of Noopept Supplement

While it is generally well-tolerated, some users may experience side effects, including:

Headaches

Headaches can occur due to increased brain activity or choline depletion. Combining Noopept with a choline source (such as Alpha-GPC or Citicoline) may help reduce this effect.

Fatigue

Some users may feel tired or exhausted after prolonged use of this nootropic. This may be due to neurotransmitter depletion, where the brain’s supply of key chemicals like acetylcholine becomes temporarily reduced.

Irritability and Mood Swings

While Noopept can regulate mood, excessive doses may lead to mood fluctuations, irritability or emotional sensitivity. These effects could be due to overstimulation of neurotransmitter systems, particularly glutamate and acetylcholine, which play crucial roles in mood regulation.

Nausea and Digestive Issues

Noopept may cause stomach discomfort or nausea. Since it is a potent nootropic, the body may take time to adjust, and some users might experience mild gastrointestinal discomfort. To minimize this effect, it is recommended to take it with meals and start with a lower dose before gradually increasing it.

Like any other supplement, it is always recommended to consult a healthcare provider before incorporating it in your routine, to avoid any risks associated with the use.

Who Should Avoid Noopept?

Individuals with pre-existing neurological conditions should consult a doctor before use.

Pregnant or breastfeeding women should avoid this nootropic.

Individuals sensitive to stimulants or nootropics may experience stronger side effects.

Is Noopept Legal?

Noopept is often categorized alongside over-the-counter supplements but is sometimes grouped with substances classified as new psychoactive substances (NPS), which are compounds that mimic traditional drugs but may not be legal to use.

While Noopept is available for human consumption in some countries without a prescription, its legal status varies depending on the region. Mostly, it is sold as a dietary supplement, while in some countries, it may require medical authorization. Always check local regulations before purchasing or using Noopept.
