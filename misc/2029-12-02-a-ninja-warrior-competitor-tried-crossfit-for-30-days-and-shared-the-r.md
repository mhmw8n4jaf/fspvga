# A 'Ninja Warrior' Competitor Tried CrossFit for 30 Days and Shared the Results

As a former Ninja Warrior champion, Ben Polson knows what it means to be in peak physical shape, and is constantly seeking new fitness challenges that push him further out of his comfort zone, from taking on boxing training to trying to pack on as much muscle as possible. In a new video on his YouTube channel, Polson embarks on a month-long undertaking which takes him right up to his physical and mental limits: CrossFit.

"I actually had to do two weeks of one-on-one coaching just to get the fundamental movements down to make sure that I wouldn't injure myself," he says. He admits that he does have some CrossFit experience, albeit 8 years ago. "Sure, I could do butterfly pullups and I could clean a bar, but that doesn't actually mean that I was strong or fit," he adds. "Which was extremely true when it came to my first WOD."

Polson's first WOD (workout of the day) consists of 10 chest-to-bar pullups, 15 pushups, 20 single leg squats, performed for as many rounds as possible in 20 minutes, with a 400 meter run after every 3 rounds. "I was pretty confident going in," says Polson. "I'm a Ninja Warrior, and I'm a calisthenics athlete, so all pullups and pushups are my specialty." However, despite a strong start, he begins to run out of steam in rounds 4 and 5, and is "feeling super dizzy" by round 6. He reaches failure and throws up 18 minutes into the workout.

"I kept coming to class, putting my body through new movements and training hard to respond to the new stimulus," he says. "I felt slow and lethargic every day, I would tire easily, and after each workout I would feel very lightheaded."

10 days into the challenge, Polson finally begins to feel the rewards, and reports feeling a sensation of euphoria after his WOD. However, just as he is starting to gain momentum in his training, his home city of Brisbane goes back into lockdown and he is forced to improvise his CrossFit workouts at home using the resources at his disposal and keeping going with bodyweight exercises and cardio.

"It can't be understated, the sheer amount of volume I've been doing in these workouts is far beyond what I would ever usually do, and it's because of the amazing structure of CrossFit WODs," he says, explaining that the way each routine is broken down, with time caps and mini challenges, makes it feel "much more managable."

At the end of the month, Polson goes back to the WOD which he was unable to complete on day 1, and tries again. "Not only did I crush my previous time, I did an additional 1 and a half rounds, and a 400 meter run, and I didn't even feel sick, I felt great," he says.
