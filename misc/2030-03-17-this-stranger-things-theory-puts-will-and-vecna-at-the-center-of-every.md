What's the Will and Vecna Theory?

Since Vecna's introduction in season 4, fans have speculated that Will is the only one who can ultimately stop the Upside Down from overtaking Hawkins.

The theory begins back in season 1, when we first see Will and his friends playing D&D. Mike introduces the Demogorgon, but the game is interrupted by Joyce right as Will makes a dice roll. Will then goes home, and realizes something is following him. While his abduction at first glance seems to be tied to the Demogorgon, some fans believe Vecna may have been the one stalking Will. Vecna is revealed in season 4 to have pulled the strings that caused Will's original disappearance, so the theory goes that he may not have sent the Demogorgon to take Will, and actually took Will himself.

Will is saved in the first season, but his connection to the Upside Down is still there. He's been possessed by the Mind Flayer in season 2, and in season 4, he can sense the Mind Flayer's presence. While Eleven's powers have helped the crew fight the Upside Down, it's a physically exhausting task, and if the past four season have shown viewers anything, it's that it'll take much more than Eleven alone to defeat the monsters in the Upside Down and close the rift for good. As Will is also closely connected to the Upside Down, it only makes sense he too could have some insight into stopping Hawkins's downfall.

His ongoing tie to the Mind Flayer has led fans to theorize Will will need to commit a great sacrifice in order to save Hawkins. CBR says Will could lose his eye, and even suspects the promotional material for season 5 shows Vecna with only one eye visible as a teaser for Will's loss. And ScreenRant broke down a similar theory in which Will will actually have to die to save Hawkins.

With more than three years passing since season 4 aired, the show's writers have had a lot of time to brainstorm how the series will end. It's possible they've even heard some of the fan theories, and tried to avoid them to ensure viewers will still be surprised. Regardless of how Season 5 plays out, the end of Stranger Things will likely be a major event akin to Game of Thrones (although hopefully a better received ending), and will capture the attention and discussion of its many fans for a long time.
