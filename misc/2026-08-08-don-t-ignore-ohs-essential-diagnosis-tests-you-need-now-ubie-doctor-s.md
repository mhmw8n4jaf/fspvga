Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome (OHS) is diagnosed when a person with obesity shows low blood oxygen and elevated carbon dioxide levels during wakefulness, without another cause of hypoventilation. Doctors typically confirm OHS using arterial blood gas analysis, pulmonary function tests, and overnight sleep studies (polysomnography) to detect associated sleep apnea.

If you're experiencing daytime fatigue, shortness of breath, or disrupted sleep, understanding your symptoms early is critical. OHS can lead to serious complications like heart failure and pulmonary hypertension if left untreated, so identifying warning signs promptly gives you the best chance at effective management. Explanation

To diagnose Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome (OHS), doctors look for signs of low oxygen and high carbon dioxide in the blood. This is often done using a blood gas analysis, which measures the levels of oxygen and carbon dioxide.

[You Need Now Ubie](https://jointrevive.xianfengchem.com/)

Additionally, a sleep study might be required to see how breathing changes during sleep. This helps in understanding if the person stops breathing or has shallow breathing when asleep.

Key steps in diagnosing OHS include:

Blood Gas Analysis: Checks for high carbon dioxide and low oxygen levels.

Sleep Study (Polysomnography): Observes breathing patterns during sleep.

Body Mass Index (BMI): Used to confirm obesity.
