# Doctors Told a 16-Year-Old He Probably Had the Flu — But He Ended Up Having Cancer

Hunter Brady, a 16-year-old from Tampa, Florida, was feeling tired and short of breath for weeks back in November. A doctor told him he had the flu, and told him to take medications and let the virus run its course.

But it turned out his diagnosis was far worse. After being rushed to the emergency room, it turned out Hunter had late-stage cancer.

WFTS-TV reports that when Hunter got to the emergency room, his right lung was collapsed and his left lung was 30 percent collapsed. By January, he had been diagnosed with stage 4B Hodgkin lymphoma. Since then, he’s had a blood transfusion, a bone marrow biopsies, surgeries, and chemotherapy. Eventually, he’ll have to have radiation treatment.

The Washington Post notes that the most common symptom of Hodgkin lymphoma is swollen lymph nodes under the skin, but people with type B lymphoma have symptoms like weight loss, fever, and night sweats. If the cancer affects lymph nodes in the chest, it can cause coughing or trouble breathing, according to the American Cancer Society. Similar flu symptoms include coughing, fatigue, and muscle aches.
