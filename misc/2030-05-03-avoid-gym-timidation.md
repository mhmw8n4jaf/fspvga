# Avoid Gym-timidation!

The gym can be an intimidating place, especially to a newcomer.

Was that a glower from the enormous dude on the flat bench—and what did you do to tick him off?

It’s an iron jungle in there, and could make any guy—especially one unfamiliar with the weight room’s customs and etiquette—feel intimidated. So we asked Men’s Health Twitter followers to tell us about their biggest weight room fears. Then we spoke to Indiana University fitness expert and kinesiology professor Jack Raglin, Ph.D., about the best ways to avoid gym-timidation.

Fear #1: Not Fitting In

Many of our Twitter followers said they felt as though they were being watched and critiqued by every pair of eyes in the gym, and so they felt self-conscious about their appearance and technique.

[Avoid Gym-timidation!](https://appetitecode.jcesqc8.com/blog/1448767842.html)

The first step to blending in, Raglin says, is to look the part. “You don’t want people to start staring at you right off because you’re not dressed the right way, which includes being too geared up or overdressed,” Raglin explains. Leave your jeans and hiking boots—or your form-fitting compression leggings and prescription goggles—in the locker room. You can’t go wrong with tennis shoes, athletic shorts, and a T-shirt.

Look up videos of exercises online so you’ll know you’re using the weights or machines properly, Raglin adds. Here’s our database of hundreds of videos to get you started.

Fear #2: Pissing Off the Natives

We received a ton of tweets from men who felt intimidated by the ripped regulars—those ultra-muscular guys who seem to live at the gym. While most of that fear is psychological, you can make enemies by interrupting the routines of your fellow weight trainers. So how do you combat both problems?

First, get out of your head: “If there’s an exercise you know you can do well, or can do using a lot of weight, do that first to gain some confidence,” Raglin says. Raglin starts his workouts on the pull-up bar because “a lot of big guys can’t do pull-ups.”

Fear #3: Women

[Avoid Gym-timidation!](https://nootropic-atlas.escortskhi.com/blog/8717495738.html)

Many men said they worried about looking out of shape or wimpy in front of fitness-minded women.

“First of all,” Raglin says, “there are a lot of women who are repelled by these giant, muscular guys. So the fact that you may not look like them won’t necessarily work against you.” In fact, she might find you more approachable.

Exercise physiologist and American Council on Exercise-certified spokesperson Mary Jayne Johnson, Ph.D., says most female gym-goers won’t judge you. “I think a lot of women would say that when they go to the gym, they feel intimidated too,” Johnson says. “Most women just want to go in, get their workout done, and get out. And as far as men go, I think women appreciate guys who are making the effort and trying to be healthy and don’t get too wrapped up in themselves and the gym culture.”

In other words, be yourself.

Fear #4: Lifting Too Little Weight

“Don’t feel like you have to reach for a heavier weight to impress anyone,” Raglin explains. “I see people all the time who use weights in a stupid fashion simply because they’re trying to make a point or show off.”

Bad form means you’re probably not working the muscle as you’d intended. For example, an improper rowing exercise will lead to a strained back, as opposed to a strong one. “It’s better to have perfect form and precise movements than to lift the heaviest weights,” Raglin says.
