IN 2023, THE Boys spin-off Gen V premiered to critical acclaim as a teen show of sorts, creating a proper live-action universe for the wildly popular (and wildly bloody) Prime Video superhero satire—which will come to an end with its upcoming fifth and final season. Gen V intertwines with the main storyline of The Boys, existing in the same world but shifting its focus to Godolkin University. This is the college where young superheroes, aka Supes, go for education and to compete for a top ranking at the school, which may get them into the elite The Seven, Vought International’s superhero team who are the focus of The Boys.

The first season of Gen V follows Marie (Jaz Sinclair), a freshman at Godolkin, who can manipulate blood and wants to become part of The Seven. However, after meeting classmates—both as friends, enemies, and even a lover—she learns more about Godolkin and the terrible secret they are hiding. That comes primarily through Luke (Patrick Schwarzenegger, pre-White Lotus), the Golden Boy of the school (and his Supe name is, quite literally, Golden Boy). He holds the top ranking at the university, has a hot girlfriend, Cate (Maddie Phillips), and a group of devoted friends that includes Andre (Chance Perdomo) and Jordan (London Thor, Derek Luh). He’s the favorite to end up in The Seven until the information that he knows about Godolkin gives him strange visions of a boy in a forest, which pushes him over the edge, ending his own life. What he leaves Marie and Andre with is vague information about something called The Woods that Godolkin is behind, and the two spend season 1 figuring out what that is.

There are a lot of moving pieces at play here, not only in Godolkin University but also with the Gen V characters in the larger Boys universe. And after a two-year wait for season 2, we all need a reminder of what happened to some of our favorite teen heroes and anti-heroes.

Marie Moreau (Jaz Sinclair)

When we first meet Marie, it’s in a flashback sequence where she’s just discovering her powers during the onset of her first period. She has hemokinesis, the ability to psychically manipulate blood, with which she accidentally kills her parents. While her sister is spared, their relationship is never the same. Years later, she’s accepted into Godolkin. She immediately befriends her roommate Emma and meets Luke, the golden boy of campus. Despite that, her troubles at the school are just beginning. When she saves upperclassman Andre at a club after he almost kills someone with his powers, she’s expelled. When she goes to confront Professor Brink, behind her expulsion, she discovers that Luke has killed him and then turns on her. Luke uses his powers to commit suicide.

That experience not only bonds all of them, but is the beginning of a tumultuous year for Marie. She becomes a known person on campus and a mentee of the dean, Indria Shetty. Luke and Professor Brink’s deaths are covered up by Vought. Marie’s relationships with her fellow students come with their ups and downs. Her relationship with Jordan is fraught—a competitive tension that ends up in a will-they-won’t-they. She and Emma are tight despite Marie’s concern about Emma’s eating disorder. When she finds out that Vought has also reached out to her estranged sister, on top of covering up Luke’s death, amidst many other problems, Andre becomes her trusted confidant in finding out what Vought is up to.

They discover a secret facility called The Woods, which holds Luke’s brother Sam, where the school has sent Supes from the school to do illegal experiments on, which included the two brothers. They also find out that a deadly virus contagious only to Supes has been developed there. Marie tries to reason with politician Victoria Neuman (Claudia Doumit) from The Boys about the virus, but she refuses. As the Supes on campus begin to uprise after those trapped in The Woods are released, Marie, alongside Andre, tries to convince their friends that this isn’t the way. She’s then attacked by Homelander for betraying her own kind, waking up in a doorless hospital room with Andre, Jordan, and Emma.

Andre Anderson (Chance Perdomo)

Andre is another upperclassman and also the son of the supe Polarity (Sean Patrick Thomas)—and Andre is being groomed to follow in his father’s footsteps. He’s besties with Luke but also secretly in love with Luke’s longtime girlfriend Cate, making a rather complicated love triangle even more so after Luke’s suicide. He becomes friends with Marie after his ability to control metal backfires, accidentally stabbing someone while Marie uses her powers to save the girl.

This, however, bonds the two of them, especially when Andre finds out about The Woods via a secret cell phone that Luke had with information about how his brother Sam was a captive there. The video also indicated that Professor Brink, whom Luke kills, was also involved in The Woods. He puts everything into finding out what exactly was behind Luke’s suicide and how they can stop The Woods. He joins forces with Marie, Cate, and his longtime friend Jordan to see how Vought is involved. While he’s discovering what insidious things are happening, he’s also exploring a burgeoning relationship with Cate despite his guilt about Luke.

One of the many things he learns about The Woods is that his father knows about it, which leaves him upset, but when his father has a seizure due to his powers, he has to be there for him. However, that gives him insight into some of the health issues he’s been having as well. Alongside Marie, the two attempt to hold Vought accountable for the crimes they’ve committed. They also try to talk down their friends involved in the Supe uprising, which leads Andre to be trapped in the doorless hospital room with Marie, Jordan, and Emma.

Tragically, Perdomo died in a motorcycle accident in March 2024 at the age of 27. Season 2 of Gen V will address this within its story, and include a proper ending for Andre.

Emma Meyer (Lizze Broadway)

When Emma joins Godolkin University, she’s already kinda famous on campus because of her influencer status and her YouTube series “Fun Sized With Little Cricket.” Her nickname echoes her superpower, where she can shrink to a tiny size, like The Boys version of Ant-Man (similar to Termite, who once made someone's penis explode from the inside). However, her power made her mother regulate her body size, which has manifested in an eating disorder. Her fame makes her somewhat of a target at the school, figuring out who wants to be friends with her for her, which includes another student disclosing personal information about Emma’s power online. However, her ride or die is Marie, her roommate, who also shows deep concern about her eating disorder. She gets involved with The Woods by way of Andre asking her to go and see what’s going on in there, since she can get small. That’s when she meets Luke’s brother Sam, who has been captive there. Emma ends up becoming close with Sam and breaks him out after accidentally killing a guard, who Sam finishes off.

When Emma wakes up at a party with all her friends and no recollection of how they got there, they all find out their memories were erased by Cate, and that she knows Sam. Once she gets her memories back, she remembers her time with Sam and the two get romantic. But he wants to confront Cate, and when he goes to find her, Emma tries to talk him down. Of course, literally being inside The Woods finds Emma helping to bring them down alongside the others, helping to try to contain the Supe uprising. Cate gets Sam on board for their pro-Supe agenda, and when Emma tries to confront him, they get in a fight. That leads Sam to end up on Cate’s side. Emma ends up getting blamed for the campus attack along with Andre, Jordan, and Marie, and she’s locked up with them.

Cate Dunlap (Maddie Phillips)

When Cate is first introduced, it’s as Luke’s girlfriend, another popular student at Godolkin. However, you can’t write Cate off as just a girlfriend, even though she also ends up with Andre. She’s a powerful telepath who can persuade others, manipulate people, and alter memories with the touch of her hand (think Marvel's Scarlet Witch or Jean Grey). When her powers first appeared, she accidentally made her brother disappear. That made her the pariah of her family, who decided to keep her locked up, wear gloves, and rarely socially interact with others in order to keep themselves and others safe.

After a visit from Shetty, Cate joined Godolkin, and with Shetty as an adoptive parent of sorts, she got directly involved in The Woods, unbeknownst to her boyfriend or his captive brother. She also worked as a spy for Shetty, controlling other Supes and erasing the memories of her friends if they discovered anything they shouldn’t know. The resentment she holds for Shetty and other humans ends with her killing Shetty and beginning the Supe uprising on campus after letting the others out of The Woods. She wrangles Sam into her cause, erasing his emotions. When the others are blamed for the killing spree on campus, Cate and Sam are named the New Guardians of Godolkin. This gets them instant fame and status. Her devotion to Homelander and Vought is shown again during season 4 of The Boys, where she’s ready to help kill anyone Homelander sees as against his pro-Supe platform.

While many of the Supes have powers that are pretty cool, Jordan’s are remarkable. One of the top students at Godolkin, Jordan, can shift between female and male forms with different powers; the former can make energy blasts, and the latter is indestructible. Jordan is best friends with Andre, Luke, and Cate, but when Marie enters the scene, things are immediately tense between them. Marie covers for Jordan after Luke’s suicide, creating an unexpected bond between them. Once they begrudgingly get to know each other, especially as they investigate what Luke left behind about the mysterious The Woods, it’s clear that the tension is romantic. They act on that as they work together to bring down Godolkin. Jordan, too, ends up locked up in the scary hospital room.

Sam Riordan (Asa Germann)

Sam enters Gen V with a bang—flipping over a car and smashing into some security guards while running through Godolkin. He’s apprehended by Andre and Marie and taken back to a mysterious room with woods in the background. It’s later revealed that Sam is Luke’s younger brother, a Supe who was diagnosed with schizophrenia and was put in a mental institution. He’s angry with his parents for even giving him Compound V, the chemical that makes people superheroes. As Andre researches what The Woods is, he discovers that Sam is in there, which then allows Emma to break him out. Sam’s power is that he has incredible super strength—to a degree no one is quite prepared for. While he ends up emotionally and romantically connecting with Emma, Sam’s schizophrenia continues to worsen due to the experiments at the facility, which give him visions and hallucinations that make people seem like puppets. Sam remains on the run for a bit, hiding out in an old drive-in where Emma continues to check in on him.

When Emma and Marie don’t remember him after he almost kills some people, it’s discovered that Cate was helping Shetty do experiments on both Luke and Sam by erasing their memories, making Luke believe his brother was dead. When he sees her again, Sam almost attacks Cate, but doesn’t because of Emma. He shacks up with Emma at school and grasps the power of being a superhero, which puts them at odds. Learning about Cate’s plan to save the people trapped in The Woods, he goes along with her and the Supe uprising despite a vision of Luke telling him to spare humans. Alongside Cate, he’s crowned one of the New Guardians of Godolkin. By the time he shows up in The Boys season 4, he’s fully pledged his allegiance to Homelander and his agenda.
