Hair loss in women is most commonly caused by genetics and hormonal changes, which often result in a condition known as female pattern hair loss (FPHL). This type of hair thinning typically appears as gradual shedding along the part line or overall reduced density on the crown. Other contributing factors can include thyroid imbalances, postpartum hormone shifts, menopause, nutritional deficiencies, and stress.

Because hair loss can stem from many overlapping causes, identifying the root issue early is key to effective treatment. Hair loss in women can have several causes, one of the most common being female pattern hair loss. This type of hair loss is usually due to a mix of genetics and hormones.

Causes of Hair Loss in Women:

Genetics: If your family members have hair loss, you might have it too. This is because of genes passed down from parents.

Hormones: Changes in hormones, like those that occur during pregnancy or menopause, can cause hair loss.

Aging: As women get older, hair can become thinner and fall out more easily.
