What Perimenopausal Black Women Experience

Perimenopause—the transitional phase before menopause—brings unpredictable hormone swings, mood shifts and changes in metabolism. Lately, many women are prescribed GLP-1 receptor agonists (like Ozempic, Wegovy and the newly approved Zepbound) to help control weight and blood sugar. If you're a perimenopausal Black woman taking Zepbound or another GLP-1, you may wonder: "Are these meds messing with my hormones—and my mood?" Here's what current research and clinical experience tell us.

Understanding GLP-1 Medications

Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) agonists mimic a hormone that stimulates insulin release, slows stomach emptying and reduces appetite. Common brand names include:

Ozempic (semaglutide)

Wegovy (higher-dose semaglutide)

Zepbound (a newer weekly semaglutide formulation)

Doctors originally prescribed GLP-1s for type 2 diabetes. Today, many perimenopausal women use them off-label to manage stubborn weight gain—an issue especially common during hormonal transition.

Hormonal Changes in Perimenopause

During perimenopause, your ovaries gradually produce less estrogen and progesterone. This fluctuation can lead to:

Irregular cycles—periods come early, late or skip entirely

Hot flashes and night sweats

Sleep disturbances

Mood swings and irritability

Weight redistribution—more belly fat, less muscle

These shifts affect not only reproductive hormones but also metabolic and stress hormones (cortisol), making perimenopause a uniquely challenging time.

How GLP-1s Might Influence Your Hormones

Current evidence suggests GLP-1 medications do not directly reduce estrogen or progesterone. However, they can indirectly affect your hormonal balance in several ways:

Weight Loss and Fat Distribution

Losing fat—especially visceral belly fat—can alter estrogen production. Fat tissue makes estrogen, so significant weight loss may lower overall levels, potentially worsening hot flashes or mood swings.

Insulin and Cortisol Regulation

Better blood sugar control may stabilize cortisol (stress hormone) peaks, sometimes improving anxiety or sleep—but in other cases, changing cortisol rhythms can temporarily disrupt mood.

Appetite and Nutrition

Reduced appetite or mild nausea can lead to lower caloric or nutrient intake. If you miss essential vitamins or minerals (like B vitamins, magnesium or iron), you might feel more fatigued, irritable or down.

Menstrual Cycle Changes

Some women report lighter or fewer periods on GLP-1s, likely due to weight loss. Others see more irregular cycles. Any sudden cycle change can amplify perimenopause mood swings.

What Black Women Specifically May Experience

Perimenopausal Black women often face higher rates of obesity, hypertension and type 2 diabetes—conditions for which GLP-1s are frequently prescribed. Unique factors include:

Genetic and metabolic differences

Higher likelihood of visceral fat accumulation

Cultural attitudes toward body image and weight loss

Barriers to healthcare access and provider bias

These factors can shape how you respond to Zepbound or other GLP-1s, and how you perceive mood swings or menstrual changes. Open communication with your provider is key.

Recognizing Overlapping Symptoms

Mood swings are a hallmark of perimenopause. But GLP-1 side effects—nausea, fatigue or low mood—can feel similar. Here's how to spot the difference:

Perimenopause mood swings

Often tied to your cycle (tracking dates helps)

Tips to Tame Zepbound and Perimenopause Mood Swings

Managing overlapping hormone and medication effects takes a multi-layered approach. Consider:

• Nutrition Focus

Small, balanced meals with protein, healthy fats and fiber

Monitor nutrient intake—especially B vitamins, vitamin D, magnesium and iron

Stay hydrated; nausea often worsens if you skip fluids

• Gentle Movement

Aim for 20–30 minutes of walking, yoga or resistance training most days

Exercise helps regulate cortisol, insulin sensitivity and mood

• Sleep Hygiene

Keep a consistent sleep schedule

Limit screens 1 hour before bedtime

Use breathable sheets; night sweats can disrupt sleep

Tracking Progress and Adjusting Treatment

Regular follow-ups help your provider fine-tune therapy. You may discuss:

Adjusting Zepbound dose (smaller increments can ease side effects)

Adding hormone-supportive treatments (low-dose estrogen or progesterone creams)

Referrals to nutritionists, physical therapists or mental-health professionals

Always report new or troubling symptoms honestly—your safety and quality of life depend on it.

Key Takeaways

GLP-1 agonists like Zepbound can help with weight loss but may indirectly affect your hormones through fat loss, insulin shifts and appetite changes.

Perimenopause mood swings and GLP-1 side effects can overlap—tracking your cycle, mood, meals and sleep helps untangle them.

Black women often face unique metabolic and healthcare challenges; advocate for culturally sensitive, personalized care.

Lifestyle support—nutrition, movement, sleep, stress management and social connection—remains essential.

For any serious or life-threatening concerns, speak to a doctor immediately.

Always speak with a healthcare provider about any persistent, severe or worrisome symptoms. Your well-being is too important to leave to guesswork—professional guidance ensures you get the safest, most effective care.
