Is sleepwalking associated with other sleep disorders?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sleepwalking is commonly linked to other parasomnias, including night terrors and confusional arousals. These conditions all fall under a category called non-REM (NREM) parasomnias, meaning they occur during deep sleep and often share overlapping causes such as sleep deprivation, stress, genetics, or irregular sleep schedules. It's not unusual for someone who sleepwalks to also experience night terrors or wake up disoriented.

Because these disorders can overlap—and sometimes signal an underlying issue worth addressing—understanding your specific symptoms is an important first step. Explanation

Sleepwalking, also known as somnambulism, is often associated with other sleep disorders. According to the medical literature, sleepwalking can occur alongside conditions such as night terrors and confusional arousals. These are all part of a group of disorders called parasomnias, which involve abnormal movements, behaviors, emotions, perceptions, and dreams that happen while falling asleep, sleeping, or waking up.

Night Terrors: These are episodes of intense fear, screaming, and flailing while still asleep. They are more common in children but can also affect adults.

Confusional Arousals: This is when a person appears to wake up but remains in a confused state, often not fully aware of their surroundings.

Research also suggests that parasomnias like sleepwalking may be connected to other behaviors during sleep, such as sleep bruxism (teeth grinding) and sexsomnia (engaging in sexual acts while asleep). Understanding these connections can help in diagnosing and managing these conditions more effectively.
