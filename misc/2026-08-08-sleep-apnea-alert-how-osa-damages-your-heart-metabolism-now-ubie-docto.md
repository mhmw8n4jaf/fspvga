Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) is a serious sleep disorder that repeatedly interrupts breathing during sleep, placing significant strain on the cardiovascular system and disrupting how the body regulates energy. Left untreated, OSA raises the risk of high blood pressure, heart disease, stroke, and metabolic conditions such as type 2 diabetes and obesity.

Because OSA symptoms—loud snoring, daytime fatigue, morning headaches, and difficulty concentrating—often overlap with other conditions, identifying the underlying cause early is critical. Explanation

Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) is a condition where breathing stops and starts during sleep. This can have serious effects on the heart and how the body uses energy.

Cardiovascular Health

OSA is linked to heart problems. When breathing stops, oxygen levels drop, causing stress on the heart. This can lead to high blood pressure and increase the risk of heart disease over time.

Metabolic Health

OSA can also affect metabolism. It can mess up how the body uses sugar, increasing the risk for diabetes. OSA might change the body's natural clock, which controls how energy is used, making it harder to stay healthy.
