Healthy Pregnancy Sleep: Managing Snoring and Comfort

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Snoring during pregnancy is common, often caused by hormonal changes, fluid retention, and weight gain that narrow airways and disrupt sleep. To reduce snoring and improve comfort, experts recommend sleeping on your left side, using supportive pregnancy pillows, elevating your head, and choosing breathable bedding. These adjustments can boost airflow, improve circulation, and ease heartburn.

However, snoring in pregnancy can sometimes signal more serious conditions like sleep apnea or preeclampsia, which require prompt attention. Because every pregnancy is different, it's important to identify warning signs early. Why Snoring Often Increases in Pregnancy

During pregnancy, your body undergoes many changes that can contribute to snoring:

Hormonal shifts (especially increased progesterone) relax the muscles of the airway.

Increased blood volume and fluid retention can cause nasal and throat tissues to swell.

Weight gain around the abdomen pushes the diaphragm upward, reducing lung capacity and increasing breathing effort.

Congestion from pregnancy "rhinitis" can block nasal passages.

Most snoring during pregnancy is mild to moderate, but if you notice very loud snoring, choking or gasping during sleep, or you feel unusually tired despite long nights in bed, speak to your doctor—these can be signs of sleep apnea, which may require evaluation.

Best Position for Snoring Pregnancy

Your sleep position has a major impact on airway comfort and circulation. Experts agree that the best position for snoring pregnancy is sleeping on your left side. Here's why:

Improves blood flow: Lying on your left side takes pressure off the inferior vena cava (a large vein that returns blood from your lower body to your heart), improving circulation to you and your baby.

Keeps airways open: Side-lying reduces pressure on the throat and allows your tongue and soft tissues to fall away from the back of your throat.

Eases heartburn: Heartburn and acid reflux become more common in pregnancy. A left-side position uses gravity to keep stomach acid from flowing back up.

Tips for side-lying comfort:

Use a full-length body pillow or pregnancy wedge between knees and under your belly to maintain spinal alignment.

Support your back with a small pillow or rolled towel to prevent you from rolling onto your back.

Additional Sleep Positions: What to Avoid

Back-sleeping: Lying flat on your back can increase pressure on your vena cava, decrease circulation and worsen snoring or backaches.

Right-side sleeping: Better than on your back, but not as optimal as the left side for circulation and reducing heartburn.

Bedding and Pillow Strategies

Beyond positioning, the right bedding can help you breathe easier and stay comfortable through the night:

High-quality, supportive mattress: A medium-firm mattress helps keep your spine neutral.

Adjustable bed or wedge pillow: Elevating your head by 6–8 inches can reduce nasal congestion, heartburn and snoring.

Nasal strips: Over-the-counter adhesive strips gently lift nasal passages open, improving airflow.

Lifestyle and Home Remedies

Healthy habits can complement your sleep setup and further reduce snoring:

Stay well-hydrated: Drinking water keeps airway secretions thin.

Gentle nasal rinse: A saline spray or rinse before bed can clear congestion.

Light exercise: Activities like walking or prenatal yoga strengthen respiratory muscles and improve sleep quality.

Avoid heavy meals, spicy foods and caffeine close to bedtime to minimize heartburn.

Elevate upper body: Using extra pillows or an adjustable bed to keep your torso elevated can reduce snoring and reflux.

When to Seek Professional Help

While mild snoring is common, certain signs warrant evaluation by a healthcare provider:

Loud, persistent snoring that disturbs your partner.

Witnessed choking, gasping or pauses in breathing.

Excessive daytime sleepiness—falling asleep abruptly or struggling to stay awake.

Morning headaches, difficulty concentrating or high blood pressure.
