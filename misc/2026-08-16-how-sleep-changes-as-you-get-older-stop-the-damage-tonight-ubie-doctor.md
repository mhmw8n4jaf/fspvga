How does sleep change as you get older?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sleep patterns naturally change with age. Older adults often experience lighter, shorter sleep, more frequent nighttime awakenings, and earlier wake times. These shifts are common and typically linked to changes in circadian rhythm, hormone production, and overall health.

However, not all sleep changes are simply "part of aging." Persistent insomnia, excessive daytime fatigue, or disrupted sleep can also signal underlying conditions like sleep apnea, restless legs syndrome, thyroid issues, or depression—each requiring different treatments. Because symptoms often overlap, self-diagnosis can be difficult and delay proper care.
