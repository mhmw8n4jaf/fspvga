What surgical options are available for correcting drooping eyelids?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Ptosis surgery corrects drooping eyelids by tightening or repositioning the muscles that lift the upper eyelid. The most common procedure is levator advancement, which shortens the levator muscle to raise the eyelid. Other options include Müller's muscle resection, frontalis sling surgery, and external levator resection, chosen based on the cause and severity of the droop.

If your eyelid is drooping, the underlying cause matters—ptosis can stem from aging, nerve issues, muscle disorders, or more serious neurological conditions. Explanation

Drooping eyelids, or ptosis, can be corrected with different surgical options. One common procedure is levator advancement. This surgery involves adjusting the muscle that lifts the eyelid, helping it open more easily. This method is often used for mild to moderate ptosis and is effective in improving eyelid function and appearance.

For more severe cases of ptosis, other surgical techniques may be necessary. These can include more extensive adjustments to the muscles or even using nearby tissues to support the eyelid. The choice of surgery often depends on how severe the drooping is and the specific needs of the patient.
