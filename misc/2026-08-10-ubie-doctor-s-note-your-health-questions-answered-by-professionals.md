Does Zinc Really Shorten a Cold? Dosage and Safety Tips

Zinc lozenges may shorten a cold by 1–2 days when started within 24 hours of symptom onset. For best results, use zinc acetate or zinc gluconate lozenges providing 75–100 mg of elemental zinc per day, divided into doses every 2–3 hours while awake, for no more than 5–7 days. Zinc is not a cure for the common cold. Key safety considerations include: - **Side effects:** Nausea and a metallic taste are common. - **Avoid intranasal zinc sprays:** They have been linked to permanent loss of smell. - **Limit high-dose use:** Long-term use can cause copper deficiency. - **Check drug interactions:** Zinc may interfere with certain antibiotics and other medications. Drowsy driving is as dangerous as drunk driving. Learn the warning signs that you need to pull over and address your sleep health.

Drowsy driving is as dangerous as drunk driving. Warning signs include heavy eyelids, frequent yawning, missed exits, lane drifting, hitting rumble strips, and microsleeps. If you notice these, pull over immediately, take a 15–30 minute nap, and use caffeine only as a short-term backup. Who is at higher risk?
