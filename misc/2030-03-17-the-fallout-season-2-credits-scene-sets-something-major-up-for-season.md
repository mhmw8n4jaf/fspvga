THERE SHOULD BE two goals to a season finale for any television show: Provide closure to at least some ongoing storylines, and plant some seeds to set up some potentially interesting new ones for the future. Throughout the course of its two seasons on Prime Video, Fallout has done a pretty excellent job at creating a post-apocalyptic world that feels huge and lived-in.

Yes, we know our main characters—Lucy (Ella Purnell), Cooper/The Ghoul (Walton Goggins), and Maximus (Aaron Moten) chief among them—but it's also become a world where it feels like we can pivot the spotlight and meet new characters in a different part of the world at any given moment... and it'll still be really interesting and probably really good.

The show's season 2 finale, "The Strip," did a good job at all of the above. We saw the end point of Cooper Howard's flashback political thriller-style story (where he met a very sinister U.S. President played by Clancy Brown), as he took the fall for his and his wife Barb's activity (Frances Turner) and was taken away. Simultaneously, we find Cooper/The Ghoul link back up with a now robotic Mr. House (Justin Theroux), discover that Barb and his daughter aren't in a vault but rather, seemingly, in Colorado, and helped Lucy out along the way. On that same note, we saw the end of Lucy's storyline with her wretched father Hank (Kyle MacLachlan) in a clever way, as he wiped his brain of memories... but is still alive. So Hank gets his comeuppance, but Kyle MacLachlan can still be on the show!

And there's also Maximus. While Maximus separated from his pal Thaddeus (Johnny Pemberton), he finally reunites with Lucy—and the two clearly have a romance kindling. But while Max is now with Lucy, Fallout cleverly diverts our attention away from what's going on with the devilish faction of the Brotherhood of Steel he left behind. More on that in a bit.

What is the Liberty Prime Alpha in Fallout?

As Fallout moves through the credits of its season 2 finale, we eventually get to a scene that puts the focus back on the Brotherhood of Steel. Dane (Xelia Mendes-Jones) brings a still-bloodied Quintus a scroll, as the leader rants on and on about how his great intentions have been squashed. Quintus the Uniter is dead, he explains, and now it's time for everyone to get ready for Quintus the Destroyer. Clearly we can tell that he's more like Quintus the fascist dictator maniac, because he pulls out some blueprint plans for something called the Liberty Prime Alpha, and clearly wants to kill tons of people who disagree with him and his view of how things in The Wasteland should be.

The Liberty Prime Alpha is a new introduction for the Fallout show, but fans of the video games will be familiar with it. The story goes that the Liberty Prime Alpha was a 40-foot-tall robot made by America in the lead-up to the nuclear war at the center of Fallout's chronology. It was was armed with tons of powerful weapons and had canned, Cold War-style anti-communist catch phrases built into its programming. In the games, the Liberty Prime Alpha was never used in the past timeline, because the United States couldn't figure out how to power it. But it eventually is found by the Brotherhood of Steel and used in gameplay, a development now echoed in the Fallout series.
