A muscle cramp is an involuntary and sudden contraction of and tightening of one or muscles that will not immediately relax. Ranging in intensity from a slight flash of pain all the way to immobilizing agony until the cramp is gone, these cramps can end in 15 seconds or last several minutes at a time and are usually visible by the way the muscle twitches under the skin. How to get rid of muscle cramps

It can often be difficult to decipher what has triggered muscle cramps. Dehydration, problems with blood flow, muscle injuries, tired muscles, and pinched nerves are just a few of the possible causes. Muscle cramps can also be triggered by vitamin deficiencies.

It’s important to visit your doctor to get to the root cause of your muscle cramps, especially if you are experiencing extreme pain. They will be able to recommend the most effective way to get rid of your cramps.

Which vitamins deficiencies can cause muscle cramps?

If you are experiencing persistent muscle cramps, a vitamin or mineral deficiency may be a contributing factor. Knowing which vitamin deficiencies can cause muscle cramps can help you get to the core of the issue. Thankfully, identifying and correcting deficiencies can cause the associated muscle cramps to vanish in short order.

Here are a few of the most commonly associated deficiencies in relation to muscle cramps:

Vitamin B

Typically used to promote blood circulation, deficiencies in Vitamin B can be a common cause of muscle cramps and can affect the efficiency of blood flow. For instance, a vitamin B12 deficiency can lead to general muscle cramps, while not getting enough vitamin B1 is a common cause of cramping in the lower legs.

Vitamin D

Vitamin D is an antiarthritic vitamin that can be effective in promoting calcification of body structure. Working in conjunction with magnesium and calcium, Vitamin D helps to control muscle contraction. Muscle cramps may be caused by an imbalance between those three components.

Some studies have shown that a deficiency in Vitamin D may cause muscle cramps and may be detrimental for muscle function; however, other studies have not found such a correlation. More studies will be needed to determine the exact correlation between vitamin D insufficiency and muscles.

Vitamin C

Vitamin C aids in the absorption of crucial electrolytes and minerals. A deficiency in Vitamin C can often lead to an electrolyte imbalance, which in turn can cause muscle cramping.

Electrolyte imbalance

Often brought on by dehydration, excessive sweating, diarrhea or vomiting, an Electrolyte Imbalance can cause cramping in the muscles as well as muscle contractions, with deficiencies in calcium, sodium, potassium, and magnesium proving especially problematic with these issues.
