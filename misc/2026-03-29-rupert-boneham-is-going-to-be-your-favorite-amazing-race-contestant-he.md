# Rupert Boneham Is Going To Be Your Favorite 'Amazing Race' Contestant. Here's Why.

The Amazing Race is set to premiere on April 17 at 9 p.m., and this season is featuring a roster of reality TV veterans from Survivor, Big Brother, and the Amazing Race itself. One of the contestants, Rupert Boneham, may be new to the Amazing Race, but to Survivor fans, he's the four-time player famous for his tie-dye shirts and long beard. Here's what you need to know about Rupert.

He's one of the top 10 Survivor players in terms of time spent on the show.

[Race' Contestant](https://repair-cascade.jcesqc8.com/)

Of the 570 contestants that have competed on the Survivor, Rupert is the sixth most often occurring contestant in show history. He's appeared in four separate seasons (Survivor: Pearl Islands, Survivor: All-Stars, Survivor: Heroes vs. Villains, and Survivor: Blood vs. Water) for a total of 104 days, but he's never made it to the final three.

He's also the first player to appear in back-to-back seasons, the first player to get the most confessionals in a season without reaching the season's finale episode, and he was the second four-time Survivor player.

He's always wearing tie-dye.

When Rupert isn't rocking his bare chest, he's wearing a tie-dye shirt. He even refers to his fans as "#TeamTieDye." And if you'd like to join Rupert and his tie-dye team, he gave a very helpful tie-dye demo on YouTube.

He's the only Survivor player to win a million dollars without ever winning a season.

The closest Rupert has ever gotten to the final tribal council is when he reached the top five on All-Stars. But in Survivor: America's Tribal Council, a special episode at the end of that season, viewers were able to vote for who they wanted to win a million dollars in a Fan Favorite Award. Rupert went up against fellow castaways Colby Donaldson, Rob Mariano, and Tom Buchanan, and he ended up winning the million dollar prize with 85% of the vote.

He runs a non-profit organization called Rupert's Kids.

Prior to his time on Survivor, Rupert worked as a mentor for at-risk kids. After winning Survivor: America’s Tribal Council, he went on to create Rupert's Kids, which is geared toward "helping youth and young adults become active members of society." The organization provides mentorship, vocational training, and educational programs.

Rupert and his wife Laura are #MarriageGoals.

Rupert often spoke fondly of Laura while he was on Survivor, and she came on the show twice during loved one visits. When she joined him on Blood vs. Water, her introduction biography read, "My husband, Rupert, is my hero. He has the most amazing strength and passion for life. He continues to help others with no expected rewards."

On Blood vs. Water, Rupert sacrificed his game for Laura when he chose to take her place in Redemption Island, and he was eliminated on Day 4 after losing his duel to come back into the game.

His victory cry is equal parts annoying and intriguing.

In 2012, Rupert was the Libertarian nominee for governor of Indiana. He said he decided to run because, "While surveying the current choices for our next Governor, I do not see anyone that has an understanding of what daily life is like for many Hoosiers nor anyone who appears to understand the harm that misguided government policies are doing to our communities." Rupert ended up losing to Mike Pence, who went on to become Vice President of the United States. Rupert and his running mate Brad Klopfenstein received about 101,868 votes, or about 4% of the total.
