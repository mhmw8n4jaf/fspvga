# U.S. Marshals Are Already 'Getting Closer' to Finding Lester Eubanks

Episode 3 of Volume 2 of Season 1 of Netflix's Unsolved Mysteries reboot is titled "Death Row Inmate," and focuses on the imprisonment, escape, and hunt for a convicted murderer named Lester Eubanks.

Eubanks was convicted and sentenced to the death penalty for the 1965 murder of a 14-year-old girl named Mary Ellen Deener.

His death penalty eventually was commuted to a life sentence, and he escaped from custody during a furlough in 1973. Since the Unsolved Mysteries episode debuted, authorities already believe they are "getting closer" to finding Eubanks.

One of the most upsetting episodes from the new batch of Netflix's Unsolved Mysteries reboot is "Death Row Inmate," which looks at the story of a man named Lester Eubanks. Eubanks is a convicted murderer, who confessed to and was found guilty of the November 1965 murder and attempted rape of a 14-year-old girl named Mary Ellen Deener. He was sentenced to the death penalty, but the execution date was moved multiple times; in 1972, the Supreme Court declared the death penalty unconstitutional, and his sentence changed from death row to life in prison.

As the Unsolved Mysteries episode describes, he eventually started what would eventually emerge as a long con. He showcased good behavior in prison, befriending and getting on the good side of guards, while also showcasing a talent for oil painting, which was well received (featured in newspaper articles, etc). Eventually, he started getting more privileges, which sometimes included the privilege of leaving prison grounds (under supervision). In 1973, as part of a prison furlough Christmas shopping trip (yes, this was somehow a real thing) he escaped. And for now 47 years, Eubanks has been on the run.

Eubanks, who would now be nearly 77 years old, is still being actively hunted by authorities. On December 7th, 2018—the 45th anniversary of his escape—he was added to the U.S Marshal's Most Wanted List, with a $25,000 reward being offered for any information leading to his arrest/capture.

He's been on the lam for nearly half a century, and might be using a different name—he's used and painted under the name Victor Young—but still has some of the same identifying qualities; he loves music, martial arts, and art.

“[All we need is] that one tipster, that one person that can bring in the last piece of the puzzle, even if they knew him a year ago, two years ago,” Deputy U.S. Marshal David Siler told Rolling Stone back in 2019. “That’s what we’re looking for. Someone who’s like, ‘I know that guy.’”

There's already an update on the Eubanks case since the Unsolved Mysteries episode debuted.

[Already Getting Closer'](https://insulin-pathways.hbsnzj.com/blog/2686716610.html)

In an interview with Bustle, Unsolved Mysteries co-creator Terry Dunn Meuer said that the show received nearly 400 tips already within only a few days of Volume 2 being released. Many of those were about Eubanks, and the impact was felt; The U.S. Marshals raised the reward for information leading to Eubanks' arrest from $25,000 to $50,000, and in a separate statement said they believe they are "getting closer" to catching him.

"He's alive," U.S. Marshal Brian Fitzgibbon told the Mansfield News Journal (through USA Today's wire). "I feel we're getting closer."

Fitzgibbon added in the interview that the service is receiving a lot of tips already helping to fill in various holes—and that no tip is too small. Still, there's specific information they need that can help to crack the case once and for all.

"There's nothing putting him in a place," Fitzgibbon said. "We're looking for any information to his life on the run or the history of his life on the run."

The identifying characteristics for Lester Eubanks are out there

While Eubanks may change his name and certain aspects of his appearance, he has a page on the US Marshals site that could help anyone to identify someone they think could be the escaped killer. On Halloween 2020, he’ll turn 77 years old, and according to his wanted poster he’s 5’10’’ and was 175 pounds at the time the poster was made, and was also listed at that weight for his appearance on America’s Most Wanted (though, obviously, that could have changed over time).

His most identifying trait might be the large scar on his right arm, as frequently mentioned in his Unsolved episode. The poster also references a scar on the left side of his face, a mole under his left eye, and a mole on the left side of his mouth.

DNA Testing Could Help Track Lester Eubanks Down

While Eubanks' current location is unknown, a November 2019 ABC News story gave insight into one way that the U.S. Marshals are looking to track him down: DNA evidence. An unidentified man who says he is Eubanks' biological son—he believes his mother was raped by Eubanks—offered the Marshals to use his DNA and test it against samples collected from crime scenes around the country as a way to try to track Eubanks' new location down.

The problem with this, though, is that “familial searches," as this sort of thing is referred, have a tricky legal standing; it's not typically allowed by the FBI, and could create sticky precedence as far as privacy concerns go.

[Lester Eubanks](https://appetitecode.jcesqc8.com/)

“Familial searches are a touchy subject,” said Natalie Ram, a University of Maryland law professor. “The federal government has a policy in place that they do not permit familial searches,” she said, adding that these policies are in place with the intention of preventing law enforcement from overstepping bounds during an investigation.

Others think there could be room for nuance, though.

"I’d argue it’s permissible to conduct familial searches at least if there are privacy safeguards in place,” said David Kaye, a Penn State law professor who says protections could be put in place so that Eubanks' son's DNA could be used on essentially a single-time basis.

The fact that new ideas and strategies are being used to track Eubanks down is encouraging nonetheless; he basically went unbothered from his first moment of escape in 1973 until his story was aired on an episode of America's Most Wanted in 1994. The search is ongoing to this day.

"We’re going to do everything within our legal means to uncover the identity that Lester Eubanks is utilizing,” Peter Elliott, the U.S. Marshal for Northern Ohio said.
