AS A DAD, everything flexes around my very cute, very demanding son. Which means there are some weeks that I don’t step foot inside a gym. One constant in our routine, however, are our twice-daily walks. Walking is as fantastic way to improve your heart health, strengthen your bones and leg muscles, and burn more calories. But I can only walk so far and so fast while pushing a stroller, so I’ve started to walk with a weighted vest—a variation of rucking that has become a popular way to amplify the benefits of walking.

Once anything becomes a trend, and weighted vest walking is undoubtedly a trend, brands emerge from the woodworks to capitalize on it. Sometimes those ideas crash and burn, but Orgeon-based Omorpho, which launched in 2017, has innovated within a saturated market, selling t-shirts and pants with small weighted beads sewn all over. The idea: By evenly distributing light weight—we’re talking one to six pounds—across your body, you can load your body for sprints, plyometrics, and calisthenics without throwing off your movement mechanics.

But in October 2025, Omorpho strayed slightly from its “micro-load” ethos, adding an adjustable version of its vest—the G-Vest Icon, which is the brand’s heaviest product—to its lineup.

One perk of being a professional writer is I get to leverage my personal fitness interests into stories, and Men’s Health asked Omorpho to send me the newly released G-Vest Icon to put through the ringer. Is it expensive? Heck yes. Is it worth it? Let's find out.

How We Tested This Vest

I mostly walked while wearing it. From when I received the vest to when I wrote this article, I accumulated 40 miles and over 70,000 steps while wearing the G-Vest Icon. That’s definitely enough mileage to draw conclusions about its comfort and performance, but not its long-term durability. I also tightened up the straps and ran the vest through a circuit of lunges, push-ups, pull-ups, step-ups, and sprints to test how it feels during higher-intensity exercises.

Fitness and Commerce Editor Charles Thorp, NASM also used the vest during jogs with his 2-year-old daughter around his neighborhood and bodyweight workouts in his local park, wearing it over a hoodie during the winter months.

Sizing and Materials

The G-Vest Icon comes in three sizes—S-M, L-XL, XXL for men, and S-M, M-L, XL for women—which are based on a combination of one’s height and chest size. For anyone who has used the original G-Vest, the sizing is the same. For reference, I’m 5’11”, 180 pounds, and have a 40-inch chest, and the L-XL fit me perfectly. I was able to tighten the Icon enough so it didn’t budge during intense workouts, and loosen it for lower impact activities like walks.

As for materials, the vest is mostly constructed with laminated polyester to synthetic rubber. Basically, its a durable rubber material that has a satin-like feel to it; it’s very comfortable on the skin. The vest is pock-marked with steel ball bearing-filled pockets to add the weight.

None of that is new to the G-Vest, though. What Omorpho did add is its “snap lock” system, which allows you to snap three additional panels—one for the back and one for each side of the vest—on the inside of the vest. Combined, all three panels can add an extra 10 pounds to bring the total vest weight from 10 to 20 pounds.

The Fit: Sleek and Form-Fitting

True to Omorpho’s brand, the Icon wears like a piece of clothing—save for the fact that it weighs 10 to 20 pounds. Given the experimentation Omorpho has done with weighted apparel, this comes as no surprise. Compared to throwing a tactical plate carrier over my head or strapping into a clunkier clip-on vest, I was able to zip into the G-Vest like I would my winter vest. Its slim profile means you can layer it like a vest, too; I had zero issues wearing it over a hoodie and under a jacket. Note: tThe vest gets thicker when you add the extra panels, so loosen the strings a bit before trying to force the zipper closed.

Let’s talk function. The vest’s back and side panels are mostly connected by elastic strings that run up the sides, which you can loosen or tighten by simply pulling the drawstring clip up or down. There isn’t a single weighted vest on the market I’ve seen that sports the same bulbous pockets as the Icon, and, you know what? They were perfectly comfortable. I trained in the vest both shirtless and clothed and the beads never caused me any discomfort.

Aesthetically speaking, the Icon is futuristic and sharp looking—the type of vest you’d expect Lukas Matsson (Alexander Skarsgård’s tech mogul in HBO’s "Succession") to be wearing as he runs in Venice. It’s almost as if Omorpho set out to design a vest so eye-catching, it’d distract you from the guffaw-inducing price tag. (See the Value section below for my thoughts on that.)

Training in the Omorpho G-Vest Icon

Setting up the vest takes no time; it arrives pre-assembled, and snapping the optional weight panels in or out adds a few seconds. Likewise, getting it on is a matter of slipping it over your shoulders, zipping it up, and adjusting the elastic side straps. The process is so simple that I’ve effectively wasted my time writing a paragraph to explain it to you. Still, I review products professionally, so no step shall be skipped.

Where the G-Vest Icon really sets itself apart is comfort. Before testing it, “comfortable” in the weighted-vest world meant doesn’t shred your underarms or scratch your chest if you go shirtless. The Icon genuinely feels like an extra layer of clothing. On walks, I often forgot I was wearing it altogether, thanks to the soft polyester shell and the way the weight hugs your torso instead of hanging off it.

That brings us to weight distribution, which is a crucial factor in how a vest feels and performs. Twenty pounds sitting high on your traps in a rucksack feels noticeably different from twenty pounds wrapped evenly around your torso. You’ll still feel the load, especially during pull-ups or sprints, but the Icon’s snug, balanced dispersion makes explosive movements and achieving a full range of motion far easier than with a bulkier plate carrier. In short: It adds resistance without getting in your way.

A few small details stood out in a good way. Despite its close fit, the Icon is surprisingly breathable, and because there are no loose plates to shift around, it stays completely silent during movement. The weight panels never shifted during my tests, even on jump squats and faster conditioning intervals. The materials also feel appropriately premium for something north of $300—you can tell Omorpho spent money on the hardware, especially the metal shoulder-strap hooks and the zipper, which never snagged.

That said, there are a few performance-related considerations before you drop serious cash. The vest doesn’t have pockets, which won’t matter much for gym-goers but could frustrate hikers or runners who rely on their vest for storage. You also can’t buy an Icon heavier than 20 pounds; for most people that’s plenty, but anyone looking to do loaded hikes or strength work may outgrow it quickly. And then there’s care: Omorpho recommends spot cleaning only and hang-drying in the shade. I haven’t had to clean mine yet, but for a pricey training tool, the maintenance feels fussier than it should.

Value

Two things can be true at once: The G-Vest Icon is easily the best weighted vest I’ve tested, and it’s priced well out of reach for most people. You can make a case for it using a little “girl math,” though.

At roughly $400 with the added G-Pack panels (for the men’s model), wearing it twice a week breaks down to just under four dollars per session. Wear it daily—on walks, during bodyweight work, even for warm-ups—and you’re looking at about a dollar a day for something you’ll use constantly. Omorpho also offers exchanges and returns, so you can always cash out if you’re unsatisfied.

The Verdict

Anyone would appreciate training in the G-Vest Icon, but it’s not for everyone. If you’re a standard gym bro—the push-pull-legs guy who wants a vest mainly to juice up walks or power through the occasional hotel-room workout—you’ll get nearly the same functional benefit from a far less expensive option. (Personally, I like the APEXUP weighted vest.)

But if you’re someone who regularly trains with a weighted vest—on runs, during warm-ups, in bodyweight circuits, or as part of your daily conditioning—you won’t regret shelling out for the Icon. It’s exceptionally well-made, comfortable, and adds load without ever getting in your way. As the saying goes: Buy once, cry once.
