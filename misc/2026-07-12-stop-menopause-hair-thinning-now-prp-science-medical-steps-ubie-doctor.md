Why Is Menopause Thinning Your Hair? PRP Science and Medical Next Steps

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Menopausal hair thinning occurs when declining estrogen and progesterone shorten the hair growth phase, while a relative rise in androgens miniaturizes follicles—causing increased shedding and finer strands. Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) therapy uses growth factors from your own blood to stimulate follicles and improve density, with studies showing a 20–30% increase in hair count over 3–6 months.

Effective treatment often combines several approaches: hormone replacement therapy (HRT), topical or oral medications, low-level laser therapy, targeted nutrition, and lifestyle changes. Because hair loss can stem from overlapping causes—hormonal, nutritional, or autoimmune—identifying the right combination matters.

How Hormonal Changes Affect Hair

During a normal hair cycle, individual hairs go through phases:

Anagen (growth): Lasts 2–6 years; most scalp hairs are here.

Catagen (transition): A brief 2–3 week phase.

Telogen (resting): Lasts 3 months; detached hairs shed and make room for new ones.

Menopause alters this cycle in several ways:

Lowered Estrogen & Progesterone

Promote hair growth and prolong the anagen phase.

Their decline shortens growth phases, so more hairs enter resting and shedding phases.

PRP for Menopause Hair Loss: What Is It?

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) therapy uses your own blood to jump-start healing and hair regrowth.

The PRP Process

Blood DrawA small vial (10–20 mL) is taken from your arm.

CentrifugationThe blood is spun to concentrate platelets in plasma.

Scalp InjectionThe platelet-rich layer is injected into thinning areas.

How PRP Works

Stimulate dormant hair follicles.

Increase blood vessel formation.

Promote collagen and healthy scalp tissue.

Cellular Activation: Encourages hair follicle stem cells to enter the growth (anagen) phase.

Anti-Inflammatory Effects: Platelets modulate local inflammation, creating a healthier environment.

What Science Says About PRP and Hair Restoration

A growing body of research supports PRP for menopausal and androgenic hair loss:

Improved Hair DensityMultiple studies report a 20–30% increase in hair count and thickness after 3–6 monthly PRP sessions.

High Patient SatisfactionOver 80% of participants in small trials noted visible improvement with minimal side effects.

Safety ProfileBecause PRP uses your own blood, allergic reactions are rare. Mild redness or swelling usually resolves within 24–48 hours.

While results vary, many women see fuller, healthier hair after a course of 3–4 treatments spaced 4–6 weeks apart, with maintenance sessions every 4–6 months.

Additional Medical Next Steps

PRP can be a cornerstone of your plan, but combining treatments often yields the best results:

Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT)

Balances estrogen and progesterone levels.

May improve hair thickness and overall well-being.

Discuss risks (blood clots, breast cancer) versus benefits with your doctor.

Topical Treatments

Minoxidil (2% or 5%): Over-the-counter gel or foam applied daily.

Pro: FDA-approved for female hair thinning.

Con: Requires ongoing use; can cause scalp irritation.

Oral Medications

Spironolactone: Anti-androgen that reduces DHT effects.

Finasteride: Off-label for women; lowers DHT—ask your doctor about safety, especially if you might become pregnant.

Low-Level Laser Therapy (LLLT)

Handheld devices or caps emitting red light to stimulate follicles.

Safe, painless option often used in combination.

Nutritional Support

Iron, vitamin D, biotin, zinc, and omega-3s support hair health.

Have blood tests to rule out deficiencies.

Scalp Micropigmentation

Cosmetic option to create the appearance of density.

Non-surgical tattoos mimic hair follicles on the scalp.

When to See a Doctor

While mild to moderate thinning is usually part of the natural transition, seek prompt medical attention if you notice:

Sudden, patchy hair loss or bald spots.

Scalp redness, severe itching, scaling, or pain.

Other alarming symptoms: chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, or sudden vision changes.

These could signal serious conditions that require immediate care.

Bringing It All Together

Menopause-related hair thinning stems from hormonal shifts that shorten hair growth phases and allow androgens to miniaturize follicles. PRP offers a science-backed, minimally invasive way to rejuvenate your scalp and encourage thicker hair. Combine PRP with hormone therapy, topical or oral medications, laser treatments, and lifestyle tweaks for the best outcomes.

Always discuss any treatment plan with a qualified healthcare professional. If you experience life-threatening or serious symptoms, seek medical help right away. Your hair (and health) deserve expert guidance every step of the way.
