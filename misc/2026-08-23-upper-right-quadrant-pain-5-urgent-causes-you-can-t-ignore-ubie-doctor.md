What are the possible causes of upper right quadrant abdominal pain?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Upper right quadrant pain most often signals a problem with the gallbladder, liver, or nearby organs. Common causes include gallstones, cholecystitis (gallbladder inflammation), hepatitis, or liver inflammation. Less frequently, it may stem from the right kidney, pancreas, or intestines. Pain can be sharp, dull, or cramping, and may worsen after eating fatty foods.

Explanation

Pain in the upper right side of your belly can have many causes. One common cause is gallbladder problems. In many cases, gallstones (small stones that form in the gallbladder) or cholecystitis (inflammation of the gallbladder) can lead to severe pain in this area, especially after eating fatty meals. The liver, which is also on the right side, can be another source of pain if it is inflamed, as in hepatitis or from other conditions. Sometimes the bile ducts, which carry bile from the liver and gallbladder, can be blocked or inflamed. Other nearby structures like the colon, right kidney, or even parts of the lung may also cause pain that feels like it is coming from the upper right abdomen. Ultrasound is often the first test used because it is good at showing problems with the gallbladder and liver.
